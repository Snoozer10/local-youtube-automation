## Production grammar to use

1. **Story before prompts.** For every canonical narration span, name the specific action, example, comparison or instruction the viewer must see. Describe the change from the preceding shot and why it matters. Reject broad mood descriptions such as "a confident person" when speech promises a concrete challenge.
2. **Episode cast before scenes.** Decide explicitly whether recurring human beats represent one illustrative participant or distinct people. Record a stable ID, immutable silhouette/face/hair/clothes, allowed expression/pose changes and the reason this character belongs in the episode. Professor remains host-free; an illustrative participant is not a presenter.
3. **Separate identity from setting.** Register each recurring character in Flow Characters using our own canonical portrait/model sheet. Keep its exact project/profile/provider receipt and accepted bytes. Attach the same verified character ingredient when it returns, even after ten scenes. Scene references remain scene-local.
4. **One dominant idea per frame.** Use a clear subject or large worked example, intentional negative space and a small number of short labels. Character + key prop + one cue is a useful starting point, not a universal layout.
5. **Alternate visual functions with the explanation.** Character situation → relevant detail → explanatory diagram → consequence/reaction → viewer task. Choose the order from the script; no mechanical camera cycle or mandatory cut frequency.
6. **Motion must reveal meaning.** Reveal the compared alternatives, trace a narrated path, highlight the relevant answer, change expression on a consequence, or move a cue to its target. A slow zoom on a generic portrait is insufficient progression.
7. **Graphics carry explanations.** Arrows, comparisons, local highlights and symbols must show relationships. Generate art without embedded writing; Python owns Arabic labels, numbers, timers and diagrams. Avoid a sequence of text-only cards unless the words themselves are the visual point.
8. **Readability at phone scale.** Inspect the encoded frame at approximately 480 px wide. Verify every essential Arabic label and instruction without zooming. Check shaping, contrast, line breaks and crop; source references also contain weak/soft frames, so do not copy their defects.
9. **Continuity does not require identical pictures.** Keep identity stable while changing pose, expression, location or viewpoint. Separate full-scene reuse from character reuse. New anonymous people are allowed only when editorial intent calls for distinct people.
10. **Use image budget as a production constraint.** Current pilot: twenty maximum, with previous spending retained. Character portrait/body generations and retries consume that same allowance. Reuse accepted character assets and deterministic local graphics; never generate one still per encoded frame.
